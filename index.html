fog/west

A live loop of fog and low cloud along the entire West Coast — Washington, Oregon, and California — from NOAA's GOES-18 (GOES-West) satellite's Pacific U.S. scan. New frames arrive about every 5 minutes; the page refreshes itself. GeoColor shows natural color by day and infrared at night; IR is the 3.9 µm shortwave band forecasters use to find fog in the dark.

Airports show live visibility from official aviation weather reports — the dot color is the reported visibility in statute miles (green ≥6, yellow 3–6, orange 1–3, red <1: dense fog). Tap one for the full report. Webcams are live Caltrans and Oregon TripCheck roadside cameras near the coast, tinted by the visibility at the nearest airport; tap for the live image.

Zoom sharpening: the loop plays at 2 km resolution. Zoom in and pause, and the current frame reloads at 1 km — "HD" appears next to the frame time.

Outlook switches the timeline to the next 18 hours: airport dots recolor to the visibility forecast in official NWS terminal forecasts (TAFs), with an "FG" tag where fog is explicitly forecast — drag the timeline to watch tonight's fog form and burn off before it happens. TAFs are only issued for larger fields, so some airports show gray in outlook. Replay re-runs actual imagery from NOAA's rolling archive (roughly the last 2–3 days) — pick a day and start hour.
