Five biplane-inspired marks in the new colors: MIT red #A31F34 primary, bright red #FF1423 reserved for live elements, MIT silver #8B959E for secondary text. Each at display size, favicon size, and locked up with the wordmark. Reply with a number.

1 · STACKED WINGS

favicon live free / fly

The purest reduction: two staggered wings and a pair of red struts leaning into the climb. Four strokes total — indestructible at 16 px, unmistakably a biplane, and the forward stagger gives it motion without drawing an airplane at all.

2 · THE N-STRUT

favicon live free / fly

Two wings joined by the classic interplane brace — which aviation literally calls the N-strut, and which draws a perfect red letter N between the planes. A biplane, an engineering in-joke, and the first letter of every US tail number (including yours) in one mark. This is the one with a story.

3 · PROFILE

favicon live free / fly

A side-profile taildragger in six strokes — fuselage, upswept tail, two wings, struts, and a red prop disc at the nose. The most literal option: nobody will ever ask what it is.

4 · THE LOOP

favicon live free / fly

A barnstormer's loop: the red smoke trail closing a circle, with the biplane — reduced to its two wing bars — punching out the top. The most "live free" of the set; pure joy, zero solemnity.

5 · THE ROUNDEL

favicon live free / fly

The stacked wings set inside a ring with a red north dot — a vintage aero-club badge. Born for embroidered patches, coffee mugs, and a sticker on the panel. Strongest as a "club" identity if friends start flying with the site.
